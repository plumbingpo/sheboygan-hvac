Keeping Your Green Bay HVAC System Running Longer

Routine maintenance is the difference between a system that lasts and one that breaks down in the middle of the season. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: fresh filters every one to three months, two feet of clearance around the outdoor condenser, a spring rinse of the condenser coil, and a professional tune-up every spring and fall, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular service, a central air conditioner typically lasts 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts acting up, like rising energy bills, rooms that won't reach the setpoint, or one repair after another, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new system during the first heat wave of the year.

If comfort problems do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A weak capacitor is a quick fix. The same capacitor left alone for a season can take the compressor with it and turn a minor repair into a full system replacement. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and miserable nights.

Typical Heating & Cooling Issues in Green Bay

HVAC systems in this area face a familiar set of enemies. Hard-running summers push compressors and capacitors to their limits, and salt air and moisture chew at outdoor condenser coils and fins, cutting efficiency years before the equipment should show its age. Clogged filters quietly starve systems of airflow, freezing evaporator coils in summer and overheating furnace heat exchangers in winter, damage that builds slowly and out of sight.

Our technicians also run into the full range of component failures in local homes: capacitors that leave the outdoor unit humming but not starting, contactors with pitted points, cracked igniters and dirty flame sensors that lock furnaces out on cold mornings, slow refrigerant leaks that leave the house lukewarm by afternoon, and blower motors worn down by years of restricted airflow. The area's mix of older homes brings undersized returns, leaky duct runs, and rooms the original system was never designed to condition.

Every one of these problems is fixable, and most are much less expensive when caught early.
